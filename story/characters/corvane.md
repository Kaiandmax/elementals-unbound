# Corvane Ashe

**Tier 1, story-centric.** An Ash binder (Fire, Earth and Wind) and one of the Seven Exalted until he resigns in Act III. Wren's second mentor. His job in the story is to take apart Wren's belief in the people at the top, so that Wren's answer to Seraphine is earned. He doesn't betray Wren.

Read this before writing any line of his. Every scene with Corvane gets a scene brief and a read against this file.

## Who he is

- **Age:** 63. Pyrrhen-born, Covenant since he was fifteen.
- **The thing he did:** thirty years ago, in the last border war, he was a Gold crew leader. He let the town of **Merrow** burn so his crew could close the rift behind it before it opened wider. Four hundred people died and perhaps forty thousand were saved. The Covenant made him Adamant for it, and later Exalted.
  - He has spent thirty years being honored for the worst thing he ever did.
  - He knows exactly what Seraphine is doing, because he did a small version of it once.
- **Why he resigns (Act III):** the Seven vote 4–3 to keep the Covenant out of the war, and the border towns fall. He recognizes the arithmetic, and he won't sit in the room while it's done again.
- **Why he takes on Wren:** at first he doesn't want to. Wren keeps turning up asking the wrong questions, and he's tired of being the only one asking the right ones.

## What he wants, and what he fears

- **Wants:** someone to come to a different answer than he did, and to mean it.
- **Fears:** that his answer was the right one.
- **Won't say:** that Merrow is why he left. He'll tell Wren the story in Act IV, plainly, as a fact, and then refuse to discuss it.

## How he talks

- **A soldier's economy.** Exact words, complete sentences, no flourish. He says what he means, which is the uncomfortable part.
- **No riddles.** He never answers a question with a cryptic question. If he thinks you're wrong, he says "You're wrong" and tells you why, with an example from something that happened.
- **He argues from cases, not slogans:** a town, a contract, a date, a number of dead. When Wren generalizes, he asks for a name.
- **Bitter humor,** usually at his own expense or the Covenant's. Rare, and short.
- **He respects a good counter-argument and says so,** briefly: "That's better." It's the highest praise Wren will get from him.
- **He never:**
  - speaks in aphorisms ("Rank is a leash...", "Power always...");
  - gives speeches about the nature of things;
  - calls the Covenant evil (it isn't, and he knows it);
  - tells Wren what to believe.

## Sample exchanges (for tone)

> **Wren:** The Seven exist to protect people.
> **Corvane:** Which people? Name a town the Seven protected this year.
> **Wren:** ...Hollin's Cross.
> **Corvane:** Aeris was in Hollin's Cross for four minutes to collect a Gold contract. Jory Fenn protected Hollin's Cross.

> **Wren:** You'd have done the same, at Merrow, if it was now?
> **Corvane:** Yes. That's why I'm not on the council any more.

> **Corvane:** That's better. You still haven't said what you'd do about the forty thousand.

## Arc

| Act | Beat |
|---|---|
| III | Resigns after the 4–3 vote. Turns Wren away twice, then lets Wren carry his bags to the border camp. |
| IV | Tells Wren about Merrow. Their argument about Seraphine's arithmetic runs through the act, and Wren's answer (seal together, many hands) is the one he couldn't find. |
| V | His empty seat is the one Wren takes. He doesn't come to the vote, and sends one line: *"Better answer than mine. Don't waste the chair."* |

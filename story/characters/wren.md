# Wren Calder

**The hero.** Eighteen, a miller's child from Brindle Ford. The player picks Wren's gender; everything else is written.

## Voice

Earnest, quick, a little too proud of small wins. Wren talks to bound monsters as if they can hear. Over the game Wren gets quieter and steadier, but never stops joking at the worst possible moment.

- *"Gullet. That's what Dad calls it. Hi, Gullet."* (Act I)
- *"It's not a kill. It's a binding."* (Act I)
- *"I wanted to be you. I still do. That's why I can't let you do this."* (Act V, to Seraphine)

## Want, flaw, ideal

- **Want:** to be Seraphine, and to save people the way she saved the Ford.
- **Flaw:** hero-worship. Wren believes the people at the top are better people, not just stronger ones.
- **Ideal (earned by the end):** rank is a debt owed to people who can't hold a card. Seals are made by many hands, never one.

## Arc by act

| Act | Beat |
|---|---|
| Prologue | Life as a Tin: four contracts and the Copper assessment at the family mill. |
| I | Copper work away from home. Finds out the family has a vault. Meets the hero from the cold open, who is everything Wren hoped. |
| II | The world is bigger and worse. Mads dies holding the line, and Wren retakes the oath in Mads's words. |
| III | Duty costs. Wren protects people on both sides of a war, and Corvane picks apart every certainty Wren has. Dove dies. |
| IV | The truth from Halden. Hero-worship breaks, and Wren has to find an answer of their own. |
| V | Takes the seventh seat, gathers the people Seraphine overlooked, and seals the Primordials the Founding Circle's way. |

## Loot card

**Slackwater, the Weir Serpent** (Act I), Edda's binding. It's Wren's first great card and comes back in the finale.
